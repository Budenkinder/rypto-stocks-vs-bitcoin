How the index works

The index answers one question: did owning these stocks do better or worse than simply owning Bitcoin? It is built in five steps, applied every week.

  1. Price each stock in Bitcoin

    Divide the stock's weekly close by Bitcoin's weekly close. The result is how much Bitcoin one share costs. If the stock rises faster than Bitcoin this ratio goes up; if Bitcoin rises faster it goes down, even when the stock gained in dollars.

    Stock $100 → $110 (+10%) Bitcoin $80,000 → $96,000 (+20%) Ratio 1.10 ÷ 1.20 = 0.917 → the stock lost 8.3% against Bitcoin
  2. Start at 100

    Each stock's ratio is rebased to 100 at its first week on the page, so a reading of 80 means 20% behind Bitcoin since then and 130 means 30% ahead. The per-stock charts show the same comparison as two lines: the price change of the stock and of Bitcoin since the stock's first week. Their headline number is the gap: a stock at −24% next to Bitcoin at −22% shows −1.9%, meaning about 2% behind Bitcoin.

  3. Combine a category with equal weight

    Each week the category index moves by the average weekly change in its members' ratios. Every stock counts the same, so the smallest company moves the index as much as the largest. The crypto-companies index starts at 100 on 11 Oct 2024 with the four stocks listed then; the treasury index starts with Strategy alone.

    Week change: stock A +4%, stock B −2% → index moves (4 − 2) ÷ 2 = +1%
  4. Let new listings join without a jump

    A stock that lists later enters the average from its second week on the page. Because only weekly changes are averaged, its arrival does not shift the index level.

  5. Measure the velocity and read the trend

    Velocity is how fast a stock is currently pulling away from Bitcoin or falling behind it, in percent per week. It is the slope of a straight line fitted through the last 13 weekly values of the stock-to-Bitcoin ratio, so a single unusual week moves it less than comparing just the first and last week. The trend label follows from it: gaining above +0.5% per week, losing ground below −0.5% per week, and mixed in between. On the two category charts, the dashed line is the 13-week average of the index, shown for orientation. All of this describes the last quarter, not the future.

    Velocity −0.8%/wk → about 10% further behind Bitcoin over a quarter (13 weeks)
Weekly data table

Stocks are weekly closes (Friday, or the last session of the week) from Yahoo Finance, with Stooq as a fallback. Bitcoin is the weekly XBT/USD close on Kraken, where the week runs Thursday to Wednesday, so it sits one to two days off the stock closes. The last stock point is the week to 16 Oct 2026; the last Bitcoin point is a partial week as quoted on 17 Oct 2026. "Refresh prices" loads the newest data at most once a day; the current week is shown as it stands at the last trade.

Start dates: Coincheck and Twenty One Capital are shown from the week their mergers closed (December 2024 and December 2025); the earlier history of the shell companies they merged into is left out. BitGo starts with its first full trading week, which misses its first two sessions. Exodus traded over the counter before moving to NYSE American in December 2024. Galaxy Digital is shown from its Nasdaq listing in May 2025; besides trading and asset management it also develops data centres.
